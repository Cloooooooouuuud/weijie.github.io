Research

I study decision-making in AI systems: when a model should continue, revise an earlier choice, ask for feedback, or change its behavior after a user instruction. I build evaluations where the relevant decision can be checked directly.

I use solver-verified or known-ground-truth tasks when possible, test failure modes before reporting a result, and run controlled multi-seed experiments with success criteria specified in advance.

Language-model reasoning First author
Under review, ICLR 2027

Decision backtracking in language models

Can a model revise an earlier decision when local improvements are no longer enough? We built solver-verified Sudoku, nurse-scheduling, and vehicle-routing tasks with controlled starting histories. Gemma4-12B changed the planted scheduling decision in 56% of episodes but reached the optimum in 0/28, separating revision from successful optimization.

with Anne Wu, Giovanni Monea, and Yoav Artzi
Human feedback Ongoing

Interactive editing with learned writing preferences

Can a writing assistant learn a user's preferences from accepted, rejected, and user-written edits? We represent preferences with interpretable weighted criteria and update them from interaction. In simulation, combining accept/reject and comparative feedback raised held-out choice prediction from 46.4% to 73.3%.

with Joyce Zhou and Thorsten Joachims
Steerability Second author
arXiv 2026

SteerEval: evaluating steerable recommenders

Do recommenders driven by natural-language profiles actually respond to steering commands? I helped build the LLM evaluation pipeline, human-grounded steering tags, sampling methods, and steerability metrics used to measure whether a requested preference change produces the intended ranking change.

Latent reasoning Ongoing

Latent reasoning with learned stopping

I train small recurrent models that solve Sudoku, modular arithmetic, and graph algorithms by refining a hidden state over multiple steps and learning when to stop. On shortest-path and minimum-spanning-tree tasks, changing the output representation raised accuracy from roughly 20–50% to above 90%.

Math+AI Lab · with Daniel Halpern-Leistner
Evaluation First author
Under review, AAAI-27

Auditing path-level chain-of-thought consistency metrics

I tested whether reasoning-path stability is a useful reliability signal using Solution Path Instability as a case study. Simple uncertainty baselines predicted errors better. The audit identified three structural problems: undefined-case defaults distort results, extractor reliability limits validity, and perturbation type can dominate the score.

World models First author
Under review, AAAI-27

Causal pretraining with real interventions

I replaced masking-based interventions with controlled changes to a physics simulator and generated matched counterfactual trajectories using shared random seeds. The experiments separate intervention detection from counterfactual prediction and show that a standard counterfactual error can be beaten by copying the input.

ARC-AGI-3, 2026

Built agents that infer rules of unfamiliar interactive games from visual observations and action feedback. A Qwen-based harness with stall detection and memory-preserving restarts matched a strong baseline with about 70% fewer actions. Reached the top 20 on the leaderboard in August 2026.

ARC-AGI, 2025
