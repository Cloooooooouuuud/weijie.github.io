B.A. Computer Science & Mathematics · Expected May 2027

I study how AI systems make decisions over multiple steps: when to keep reasoning, when to revise an earlier choice, and how to change behavior after human feedback.

I build controlled evaluations where these decisions can be checked directly, and learning methods that use feedback to improve behavior. My recent work studies decision backtracking in language models, preference learning from lightweight interaction, latent reasoning with learned stopping, and failure modes in reasoning metrics.

At Cornell, I work with Thorsten Joachims, Yoav Artzi, and Daniel Halpern-Leistner.

Research

My work centers on three related questions: how models revise and allocate computation during reasoning, how people can steer model behavior with lightweight feedback, and how to evaluate these behaviors without relying on misleading proxies.

01

Reasoning and revision

When should a model continue, stop, or change an earlier decision?

I study this with solver-verified tasks and recurrent models whose intermediate decisions can be tested directly. In decision-backtracking experiments, models often revise a poor earlier choice without reaching the optimum; in latent-reasoning work, output representation and stopping behavior strongly affect success.

02

Human feedback and steerability

How can a system learn what a user wants from language and a small number of decisions?

I work on systems that learn interpretable preferences from accepted, rejected, comparative, and user-written edits, and on evaluations that test whether natural-language steering changes a recommender in the requested direction.

03

Reliable evaluation

How do we know that an apparent improvement reflects the model rather than the metric?

I audit metrics with simple baselines, alternative extractors, matched perturbations, and known-ground-truth tasks. This has exposed failure modes in path-level chain-of-thought consistency metrics and in standard counterfactual error for world models. I also use trajectory analysis in ARC-AGI-3 to separate perception, reasoning, and execution failures.
